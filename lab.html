🕵️ What Looks Like the Main Idea, But Isn't (The 5 Classic Impostors)

Test writers use true statements from the passage to trick students into picking the wrong option.

1. The Hook Bait

"The opening status quo"

Sounds general and grand in lines 1-2, but the author pivots against it with "However".

"E-readers make novels more accessible than ever." (Hook, refuted by P1 thesis!)
2. Single Topic Sentence

"The Too-Narrow Trap"

A strong claim that governs only one body paragraph, not the entire essay umbrella.

"Paper books eliminate app notifications." (Only covers P3, misses comprehension!)
3. Memorable Detail / Stat

"The Familiar Fact"

A striking laboratory number or study. Evidence supports an argument, but is never the main idea.

"Particles under 5mm bypass sedimentation tanks." (Raw proof, not the main takeaway!)
4. Broad Topic Label

"The Missing Claim"

A noun phrase stating what the text is about, but completely omitting the author's verdict or stance.

"AI tools in secondary classrooms." (Just a title label! Has no verb or judgment.)
5. Discussion Half-Truth

"The One-Sided Pick"

Picking solely the Pro side (or solely the Con side) of a balanced discursive text.

"High-speed rail drastically cuts carbon." (Ignores rural debt risks in P3!)
The Umbrella Test: Does the candidate sentence cover Paragraph 1, Paragraph 2, Paragraph 3, AND the Conclusion? If it only fits one paragraph, it's an Impostor.
1. TOPIC

Where: Title / Opening noun

1–4 words. No verbs, no claims, no judgment.

NOT: A full sentence.

2. THESIS

Where: Last sentence of P1

The author's core debatable stance after the pivot.

NOT: The opening hook bait.

3. ARGUMENT

Where: 1st sentence of P2/P3

Major supporting pillar governing a whole paragraph.

NOT: Just a raw number or study.

4. CLAIM & DETAIL

Where: Middle of P2/P3

Claim: Sub-assertion needing proof. Detail: Numbers, facts, studies.

NOT: The text's main idea.

5. RESTATING / REC

Where: Paragraph 4

Analytical: Restates thesis. Hortatory: Mandatory policy action.

NOT: A brand new argument.
